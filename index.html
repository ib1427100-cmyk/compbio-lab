Plain-language glossary

Protein sequence: the order of amino-acid building blocks in a protein. FASTA: a text format for sequences. Reference sequence: the standard, normal form of a protein. Variant / mutation: a change in that sequence. Biomarker: something measurable that can signal a biological state. Classifier: a program that sorts examples into groups using patterns in data. Simulated: made up for teaching.

Module 1 / Proteomics

Protein Structure Predictor

Purpose: look at what can be calculated from one protein sequence.

DNA provides the instruction manual, but proteins are the machines that do the actual work in our bodies. Their function is entirely determined by their 3D shape. We've built an interactive tool to predict how raw biological text folds into physical shapes.

1. The Rules

Each amino acid letter naturally wants to coil into a spiral (Helix) or lay flat (Sheet). We look up these microscopic preferences.

2. The Context

Letters don't act alone. We write algorithms to analyze groups of 5 or 6 letters, because neighbors force each other into structure.

3. The Output

If the math crosses a threshold, the code snaps into a Helix (H) or a Sheet (E). If it's mathematically unstable, it stays a Coil (C).

Live Prediction Engine

Type or paste a sequence below. Everything below (biomarker panel, and the cancer evidence card in Module 2) is computed from this exact sequence. Accepts NCBI FASTA, GenPept or raw text.

Limitation: this screen reads the sequence only. It cannot tell whether a person has cancer, because healthy people carry the same normal-form proteins. It recognises a limited list of lung-cancer proteins and mutation hotspots.

Module 5 / Biological Age

Biological Age Estimator illustrative

Purpose: see why biological age can differ from age in years, using a simple teaching calculation.

Enter biomarker values to see an illustrative estimate. This is not a validated biological-age model.

How the estimate is calculated

  • Each marker is compared with a typical healthy reference value. The difference (in steps of 8 lymphocyte points, 1 RDW %, a 3-fold CRP change, and so on) is limited to ±2 steps.
  • Each step moves the estimate by a fixed number of years (0.75 to 1), up for inflammatory or stress markers and down for markers of good reserve. The total is capped at ±15 years and added to chronological age.
  • The reference values and weights were chosen for teaching and were not fitted to any dataset. They have no demonstrated accuracy. Real approaches (Levine et al., Aging 2018, Phenotypic Age from nine clinical biomarkers; Horvath, Genome Biol 2013, DNA-methylation clock) are trained on large cohorts.

Module 4 / Biomarker Intelligence

Biomarker Dashboard

Purpose: learn what common biomarkers measure and why none of them alone shows cancer.

Research biomarkers across ageing, immunity, blood and lung cancer. Most are not specific to lung cancer: read each limitation. References are general reviews; verify before formal citation.

Terminology: a gene (KRAS, EGFR) is a DNA segment; a protein (KRAS, EGFR) is its product; a mutation (p.G12C, p.L858R) is a change in either; a circulating biomarker (CEA, CRP, IL-6) is a molecule measured in blood. Gene mutations are found in tumour DNA, not by measuring blood proteins.
